- A type of X-ray of the chest

If a question asks 'why did this disease occur?', it is asking about:
* Aetiology
- Pathogenesis

@ Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2

E: Conduction is heat transfer through direct contact between surfaces. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

Which response helps conserve/produce heat when core temperature falls?

- Increased evaporation from the lungs only

- Raising sweat gland output

- A bacteriostatic effect
E: Hyperthermia reflects overwhelmed heat-loss/ excessive heat gain without a raised set-point. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

Nociception is best described as:
* Neural detection/processing of noxious stimuli that can give rise to pain perception
- The same as normal touch in all respects
- A drug distribution phase
- A type of imaging with radiotracers
- Loss of function after inflammation only
E: Nociceptors transduce damaging stimuli; pain is the perceived experience. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

Immediately after stubbing a toe, a sharp 'first pain' is carried mainly by:

- Slow C fibres only
- Photoreceptor fibres
- Motor fibres to muscle

Which correctly contrasts acute and chronic pain?
* Acute pain - recent onset, often linked to injury and protective; Chronic pain - prolonged/persistent beyond the acute episode
- Acute pain - always lasts years; Chronic pain - always lasts seconds
- Acute pain - cannot be assessed; Chronic pain - only a sign, never a symptom

@ Lecture 4: Pharmacology
Which branch pairing is correct?
* Pharmacokinetics - what the body does to the drug; Pharmacodynamics - what the drug does to the body
- Pharmacokinetics - receptor effects only; Pharmacodynamics - excretion only
- Both terms mean drug naming
- Pharmacokinetics - manufacture of tablets; Pharmacodynamics - packaging
- Neither relates to mechanism of action
E: ADME belongs to kinetics; effects/mechanisms belong to dynamics. (Lecture 4: Pharmacology)

A drug given intravenously bypasses absorption and first-pass loss, so its bioavailability is conventionally taken as:
* Essentially complete (the reference for other routes)
- Zero in all cases
- Unrelated to systemic circulation
- Determined only by tablet flavour
- The same as its excretion rate
E: Oral bioavailability is reduced by incomplete absorption and first-pass metabolism. (Lecture 4: Pharmacology)

After entering the bloodstream, a drug that leaves plasma and enters tissues is in the phase called:
* Distribution
- Liberation from packaging only
- Excretion only
- Reception
- Incubation
E: Distribution depends on blood flow, lipid solubility, plasma-protein binding and tissue barriers. (Lecture 4: Pharmacology)

Metabolism (biotransformation) in ADME mainly:
* Chemically alters the drug, often in the liver, usually making it easier to excrete and/or inactivating it
- Moves the drug from blood to tissue without chemical change
- Is the same as taking the tablet orally
- Defines the karyotype

E: Metabolism can also activate prodrugs, but its classic role is chemical transformation. (Lecture 4: Pharmacology)

Excretion differs from metabolism because excretion is:

- Binding to a receptor
- Release from a capsule
- Movement into tissues
E: ADME separates chemical biotransformation from elimination from the body. (Lecture 4: Pharmacology)

According to Clark's receptor theory (1937), increasing the fraction of receptors occupied by an agonist would generally:

If a drug works by blocking an enzyme, that statement describes its:
