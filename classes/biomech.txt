* Describes how a body moves without considering the forces causing the motion
- Studies the forces that cause or resist motion
- Studies only muscles
- Measures balance only

* Rigid bodies linked by joints, moving in 3D space under internal and external forces

@ Kinetics, levers & energy
Moment of inertia (I = m·r²) means that:
* Mass farther from the axis makes a segment harder to rotate
- Mass distribution is irrelevant
- Only mass matters
- Heavier objects rotate more easily
E: Bending the knee during swing reduces I so the leg moves faster. (Concepts II)

Newton's 2nd law for rotation states:
* T = I·α
- F = m·v
- P = m·v
- W = m·g
E: Linear F = m·a; rotational torque = moment of inertia × angular acceleration. (Concepts II)

A 10 N force on a 0.62 kg basketball produces about 16 m/s². A billiard ball of one quarter the mass would receive:
* Four times greater acceleration
- The same acceleration
- One quarter the acceleration
- Twice the acceleration
E: a = F/m. (Concepts II)

Torque is zero when:
* The line of action of the force passes through the axis of rotation
- The force is very large
- The moment arm is maximal
- The joint is at 90°
E: T = d × F; d = 0. (Concepts II)

Holding a 50 N load, elbow torque at 90° is 15 N·m (d = 0.30 m). At 30° (d = 0.08 m) it is:
* 4 N·m
- 15 N·m
- 50 N·m
- 0 N·m
E: Moment arm changes during movement. (Concepts II)

Which is an EXTERNAL force?
* Ground reaction force
- Muscle force
- Ligament force
- Joint reaction force
E: Internal: muscle, ligament, joint reaction. External: gravity, GRF, external load, wind, friction. (Concepts II)

In a first-class lever:
* The fulcrum lies between effort and resistance
- The resistance lies between fulcrum and effort
- The effort lies between fulcrum and resistance
- There is no fulcrum
E: Course example: supraspinatus/upper limb. (Concepts II)

The course example of a second-class lever (effort arm > resistance arm) is:
* Tibialis anterior lifting the foot
- Biceps flexing the elbow
- Supraspinatus abducting the arm
- A seesaw
E: Concepts II. (Wheelbarrow is the everyday example.)

The biceps flexing the elbow is a:
* Third-class lever with mechanical disadvantage
- First-class lever with advantage
- Second-class lever
- Not a lever
E: Effort between fulcrum and resistance; MA < 1 but more speed and ROM. (Concepts II)

Mechanical advantage is the ratio:
* Resistance / Effort (= effort arm / resistance arm)
- Effort / Resistance
- Mass / acceleration
- Torque / time
E: MA > 1 = advantage. (Concepts II)

The patella improves knee extension by:
* Increasing the quadriceps lever arm, reducing the mechanical disadvantage
- Reducing the quadriceps moment arm
- Absorbing all compressive force
- Converting it to a first-class lever
E: Example MA 0.1 → 0.3. (Concepts II; Knee lecture: patella increases quadriceps moment arm.)

Leaning the trunk forward when climbing a step:
* Reduces the resistance moment arm and the quadriceps force needed
- Increases quadriceps force needed
- Makes no difference
- Converts the movement to isometric
E: Example: required quadriceps force drops from >658 N to >278 N. (Concepts II)

Momentum is:
* Mass × velocity (vector)
- Mass × acceleration
- Force × distance
- ½ m v²
E: Angular momentum L = I·ω. (Concepts II)

Impulse equals:
* Force applied over time = change in momentum
- Force × distance
- Mass × height
- Power × time²
E: J = ∫F·dt = ΔP. (Concepts II)

Mechanical work is:
* Force × distance – a scalar
- Force × velocity – a vector
- Mass × acceleration
- Torque / time
E: Work is positive if force and displacement in same direction. (Concepts II)

Negative muscular work corresponds to:
* Eccentric contraction – absorbing energy and decelerating the segment
- Concentric contraction
- Isometric contraction
- Muscle relaxation
E: Concepts II.

In an isometric contraction:
* Work and power are zero even if force is high
- Power is maximal
- Work is maximal
- Velocity is maximal
E: No displacement → no work or power. (Concepts II)

Isokinetic testing at low speeds (e.g. 60°/s) mainly assesses:
* Strength (maximal torque)
- Power at high speed
- Balance
- Flexibility
E: Higher velocities (e.g. 180°/s) assess power. (Concepts II)

- Not at all

In walking, potential and kinetic energy are:

- In phase
- Both constant
- Both zero

The CoM is at its highest point, with maximal potential energy, during:
