@ Research foundations
A research protocol is:
* The written plan of the study, setting out its structure
- The published results paper
- The ethics committee's approval letter
- The statistical software output
E: Also used for grant funding and IRB approval; scientifically it organises research logically and efficiently. (Lesson 1, anatomy of research)

The "physiology of research" refers to:
* How research works – drawing inferences from findings
- The study's anatomical structure
- Muscle physiology experiments
- The budget
E: Anatomy = what it is made of (study plan); physiology = how it works. (Lesson 1)

Internal validity is:
* The degree to which the investigator draws correct conclusions about what actually happened in the study
- How well results generalise to people outside the study
- The reliability of a measurement tool
- The ethics of the study
E: Inference #1. (Lesson 1)

External validity (generalisability) is:
* The degree to which conclusions apply to people and events outside the study
- The accuracy of conclusions inside the study
- Inter-rater agreement
- The sample size
E: Inference #2. (Lesson 1)

"Empirical" means:
* Based on observation or experience
- Based on opinion
- Based on theory only
- Based on tradition
E: Empirical evidence can only come from research. (Lesson 1)

Which order of the scientific method was presented?
* Observe → research → hypothesis → test → analyse/conclude → share results
- Hypothesis → share → observe → test
- Publish → observe → conclude → test
- Test → hypothesis → observe → research
E: Example: movement sonification for walking after stroke. (Lesson 1)

Which databases were named for the research step?
* Google Scholar, PubMed and PEDro
- Wikipedia and YouTube
- Instagram and Facebook
- Only textbooks
E: Lesson 1, scientific method step 2.

A research question is:
* The uncertainty the investigator wants to resolve by performing the study
- The study's title
- The statistical test chosen
- The conclusion of the study
E: Lesson 1.

Which is a required property of a good research question?
* It must be testable and lead to a falsifiable hypothesis
- It must already have a known answer
- It must be impossible to measure
- It must avoid ethics review
E: It should have a real answer, be testable, lead to a falsifiable hypothesis, and be ethical and relevant. (Lesson 1)

A "falsifiable" hypothesis means:
* There must be a possible negative answer to it
- It can never be wrong
- It is based on false data
- It needs no experiment
E: Lesson 1.

In Lesson 1's description of an experiment, the control group is:
* What the experimental group is compared against
- The group receiving the new treatment
- The researchers
- The ethics committee
E: An experiment includes an experimental group and a control group. (Lesson 1)

If experimental results concur with the hypothesis's deduced consequences:
* The hypothesis is verified; otherwise it is falsified
- The hypothesis is automatically proven forever
- The study must be stopped
- The control group is removed
E: Lesson 1.

For a new investigator without an experience base, a recommended source of research questions is:
* Carrying out a systematic review
- Guessing
- Repeating a colleague's study exactly
- Avoiding the literature
E: Others: careful observation of patients, staying open to new ideas and technologies. (Lesson 1)

What does PICO stand for?
* Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome
- Problem, Investigation, Conclusion, Objective
- Patient, Illness, Cause, Outcome
- Population, Incidence, Cost, Outcome
E: A format to help define the research question. (Lesson 1; RP Guidelines)

In "virtual reality vs conventional rehabilitation for upper-limb function in chronic stroke", the Comparison (C) is:
* Conventional rehabilitation training
- Virtual reality
- Upper-limb function
- Patients with chronic stroke
E: P = chronic stroke patients, I = VR, O = upper-limb functionality. (Lesson 1 example)

What does SMART stand for in objectives?
* Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
- Simple, Medical, Accurate, Reliable, Tested
- Statistical, Methodical, Applied, Randomised, Trial
- Specific, Meaningful, Accurate, Rapid, Tested
E: Lesson 1.

Objectives in the research protocol should start with:
* A verb in the infinitive (e.g. to assess, evaluate, determine)
- A verb in the past tense
- A question
- The conclusion
E: Structure: verb – intervention – outcome – population; one objective per outcome. (Lesson 1)

How many objectives should each study outcome have associated with it?
* Each outcome should have an associated objective
- None
- Only the primary outcome needs one
- Exactly five
E: Lesson 1.

The stroke epidemiology slide stated that stroke affects about:
* 15 million people worldwide every year, with ~30% first-year mortality
- 1,000 people a year
- 150 million with no mortality
- 15 thousand people a year
E: Severe disability in two-thirds of survivors (Pu et al., 2023). (Lesson 1)

@ Research Protocol guidelines
The RP assignment consists of:
* A brief introduction and an extensive methods section of a research protocol
- A full results and discussion
- A literature review only
- A case report
E: RP Guidelines.

The knowledge-gap (relevance) section may be at most:
* Two paragraphs
- Five pages
- One sentence
- Ten paragraphs
E: RP Guidelines.

The hypothesis section must include:
* A null and an alternative hypothesis
- Only the expected p-value
- Only the conclusion
- The sample size
E: RP Guidelines.

In the study design section you must explain:
* The study arms and which guidelines you will follow to describe procedures
- The results obtained
- The authors' CVs
- The budget only
E: Plus a flow chart. (RP Guidelines)

The randomisation section must state:
* The method used and who will perform it
- Only that randomisation "will be done"
- The patients' names
- The final results
E: RP Guidelines.

The blinding section must describe:
* The procedure used and who will be blinded
- The colour of the documents
- Only the statistical test
- Inclusion criteria
E: RP Guidelines.

Which element describes the MATERIALS used in the intervention?
* WHAT
- WHO PROVIDED
- HOW
- WHERE
E: Brief name, what, who provided, how, where, when and how much. (RP Guidelines)

The "HOW" element of the intervention describes:
* Mode of delivery (face-to-face or tele-rehabilitation) and individual vs group
- The location
- The materials
- The provider's training
E: RP Guidelines.

The "WHEN AND HOW MUCH" element includes:
* Number of sessions, schedule, duration, intensity and dose
- The provider's qualifications
- The building name
- The research question
E: RP Guidelines.

The expected change in the main outcome for the sample size should take into account the:
* Minimal clinically important difference (MCID)
- Researcher's preference
- Number of authors
- Journal impact factor
E: If MCID is not established, use previous similar studies; source suggested: Rehabilitation Measures Database (sralab.org). (RP Guidelines)

When choosing statistical tests in the data analysis section you must consider:
* The type of variable, the number of groups and the number of assessment time points
- Only the sample's age
- The colour of the graphs
- The software price
E: RP Guidelines.

The ethics section must reference:
* The Declaration of Helsinki and the Ethics Committee, with a mandatory Informed Consent form
- Only the university logo
- Patient names
- Nothing – ethics is optional
E: RP Guidelines.

References in the RP must follow:
* Vancouver style
- APA style
- Harvard style
- Any style
E: RP Guidelines.

The RP must be written in which verb tense?
* Future tense
- Past tense
- Present perfect
- Conditional
E: It is a protocol for a study not yet performed. (RP Guidelines)

Which font and spacing are required?
* Arial, Calibri or Times New Roman 10–11, line spacing 1.5
- Comic Sans 14, double spacing
- Any font, single spacing
- Times 12, spacing 2.0
E: Titles may use a different size; text in black. (RP Guidelines)

Which pages must be numbered?
* Every page except the title page
- Only the title page
- No pages
- Only odd pages
E: RP Guidelines.

What happens if information is placed in the wrong section?
* Points will be deducted
- Nothing
- It earns bonus points
- The RP is automatically failed
E: RP Guidelines.

Late uploads of the RP to Moodle are:
* Not accepted

- Accepted any time
- Accepted by email

The RP front page and index must be:
* On separate pages

E: Front page: university, students, teacher, course, date; plus a summary page. (RP Guidelines)
