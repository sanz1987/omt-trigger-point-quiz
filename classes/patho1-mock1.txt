@ Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2
Which set contains the cardinal signs/symptoms of inflammation?

- Pallor, coldness, dryness, numbness and atrophy
- Bradycardia, hypotension, coma, paralysis and cyanosis
- Itching only, with no other feature
E: The classic cardinal features are rubor, calor, tumour/swelling, dolor and functio laesa (loss of function). (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

A hot, red, swollen ankle after a sprain most directly illustrates:

- Trisomy

Heat balance is maintained when:

- Sweating occurs without any heat loss

Sitting near, but not touching, a cold window and losing heat to it is mainly:
* Radiation
- Conduction
- Convection
- Evaporation
- Metabolism
E: Radiation transfers heat to cooler objects without direct contact. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

Which correctly paired heat-transfer example is correct?
* Ice pack on skin - conduction; fan moving air - convection
- Ice pack on skin - evaporation; fan - radiation
- Sweating - conduction; shivering - evaporation
- Sun warming skin - conduction only; ice pack - convection
- Fan - conduction; direct contact - radiation
E: Conduction needs direct contact; convection uses a moving fluid such as air. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

When body temperature rises above normal, the expected physiological response is:
* Cutaneous vasodilation and sweating
- Cutaneous vasoconstriction and shivering
- Chills with piloerection only
- Reduced sweating and heat conservation
- A raised hypothalamic set-point in every case
E: Vasodilation brings warm blood to the skin and evaporation from sweating removes heat. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

Which statement distinguishes fever from hyperthermia?
* In fever the hypothalamic set-point rises; in hyperthermia it does not, but heat gain/loss fails
- Fever never involves the hypothalamus; hyperthermia always raises the set-point
- Hyperthermia is always caused by infection; fever never is
- Fever is below normal temperature; hyperthermia is normal temperature
- They are identical in mechanism and treatment in all cases
E: Fever is a regulated rise in set-point; hyperthermia is overheating despite a normal set-point. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

Which temperature-change pairing is correctly matched?
* Rising fever set-point - chills/shivering; falling set-point - sweating/vasodilation
- Rising set-point - sweating only; falling set-point - shivering only
- Rising set-point - vasodilation with no chills; falling - vasoconstriction
- Both phases - apnoea
- Both phases - loss of hypothalamic control
E: The body shivers to reach a higher set-point, then sweats and vasodilates as it falls. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

A pinprick is first detected in pain perception by:
* Nociceptors (free nerve endings responding to noxious stimuli)
- Pacinian corpuscles only
- Photoreceptors
- Thermoreceptors that never signal pain
- Motor end plates
E: Nociceptors detect potentially damaging mechanical, thermal or chemical stimuli. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)

Which fibre pairing is correct?

- Both fibres - identical speed and pain quality
- A fibres - only temperature; C fibres - only proprioception
- C fibres - no role in nociception
E: Myelinated A-delta fibres conduct rapidly; unmyelinated C fibres conduct more slowly. (Lecture 2: Health and Disease - Part 2)
